      What's here. The proposal is Jason Edelman's maximal reading of Ghent Streets' amendment: every parcel in the League's area that NFK2050, Norfolk's comprehensive plan, would let hold retail (along arterial and collector streets in its Sustain place type, p. 69; on the medical campus, pp. 80–81; in the Regional Activity Center, p. 82), plus HC-G2's corner lots, plus every lot on Graydon Avenue and Blow Street. Where there's a building to adapt, its ground floor holds one of the uses the amendment would permit by right, at most 3,500 sq ft, in Policy Brief No. 2's mix: half cafés and restaurants, three tenths shops, a fifth services.

      Doors and windows stay where they are. This is adaptive reuse: the buildings are adapted, not remodelled. A house keeps its door and windows, and the new use shows through them, with a sign over the door, awnings, and tables in the yard. Only a building that's already commercial gets a storefront.

      What's whose. The City of Norfolk's open data: the League's boundary, zoning, parcels, building footprints and heights, street centerlines and their class, addresses, assessments, the tree inventory, and the assessor's photos. NFK2050's place types are the City's own map (its ArcGIS layer is titled DRAFT). Data inferred where not provided: roof shapes (a hip or gable on houses that nearly fill a rectangle, flat otherwise), windows and doors (laid out in bays), sidewalks, tree heights and crowns (from trunk diameters), and the few heights the City's data lacks. Ours: which business is where within the brief's mix, their signs and what they put out, and the people.
