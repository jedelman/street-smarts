Eastside Commons

73 acres at Military Circle, Norfolk. The current parcel fabric — a dying mall and its parking lots — alongside the proposed Eastside Commons scheme generated by the EC_FieldSolver: 19 designated parcels organizing 2,400–4,000 homes, civic spaces, a sponge park, and a commons-owned main street.

Below, three opinions look at the place from three angles. They are opinions — encoded interpretations of Christopher Alexander (via Salingaros 2025) and of the Eastside Commons coalition's politics. None of them claim to measure aliveness. What's accurate here is not the numbers; it's the record of what you decide after looking at them.

What is this?

street-smarts is a Rust + WebAssembly library that runs entirely in your browser. It takes neighborhood data and runs three opinions against it:

  • Levels of Scale — Alexander's 1st fundamental property of wholeness, after Salingaros 2025. Asks: does this place have variety of sizes, with ratios in the 2–5× band?
  • Strong Centers — Alexander's 2nd property. Asks: does this place have a hierarchy of named centers spread across it?
  • Ownership Pattern — an activist opinion (not Alexander's). Asks: who owns this land? Commons / CLT / cooperative / public, or private?

Activist opinions are not averaged with geometric ones. Alexandrian beauty cannot be traded for displacement. The two choruses are surfaced separately.

Each opinion explicitly declares what it doesn't see. An opinion that can't speak to a case returns "no view" — a first-class output. The library refuses to fake confidence.

Disagreement is the point. When opinions agree, the interesting question is what they're all missing. When they disagree, the interesting question is which voice belongs in this conversation.
