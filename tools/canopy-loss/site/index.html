Ghent Canopy Loss

Norfolk, VA — satellite analysis, 2011–2023

Norfolk's Ghent neighborhood has a mature urban tree canopy that residents value deeply. The city has not updated its tree survey in years. So we looked at the data ourselves.

Using publicly available USDA NAIP aerial imagery at 1-meter resolution, we compared Ghent's tree canopy between June 2011 and October 2023. Drag the slider to compare.

What the data shows

The headline number — near-zero net change — masks a more turbulent reality. 35 acres of vegetation were lost and 34 acres gained, meaning roughly a third of Ghent's canopy turned over in twelve years. The trees standing in 2023 are often not the same trees that stood in 2011.

The change map shows the spatial pattern clearly. Red clusters along the commercial corridor (Monticello Ave / Colley Ave) and scattered through residential blocks mark where mature canopy was removed — likely for new construction, parking, or storm damage. Green patches appear on the east side and in yards where young trees have grown in or landscaping has filled gaps.

The most concerning pattern: only 77 of Ghent's 106 original canopy acres are still standing. That means 29 acres of mature tree canopy — trees that took decades to grow — have been replaced. The 34 acres of new vegetation are predominantly young growth that provides less shade, less stormwater absorption, and less habitat than the canopy it replaced.

Caveats

The 2023 image was captured in October vs June for 2011. Deciduous trees in Norfolk (USDA zone 8a) still carry most of their leaves in early October, but NIR reflectance is somewhat lower in fall. The rank-based comparison used here mitigates seasonal effects by comparing each pixel's relative greenness within its own year's distribution, but a same-month comparison would be more definitive.

NAIP imagery is available for seven vintages covering Ghent (2011, 2012, 2014, 2016, 2018, 2021, 2023). The USDA changed its radiometric processing between years, making raw NDVI values incomparable across vintages. The percentile-rank approach used here is robust to these calibration differences, but the visual before/after comparison remains the most trustworthy evidence.

Method

Imagery was accessed from the Microsoft Planetary Computer NAIP collection via STAC API at 0.5m resolution. Each year's best-overlapping scene was selected for the Ghent bounding box (-76.305, 36.871 to -76.285, 36.878). Red (Band 1) and NIR (Band 4) were used to compute NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index).

Change detection uses percentile-rank differencing: each year's NDVI values are independently ranked from 0 (least green pixel) to 1 (most green pixel). Pixels where the rank shifted by more than 15 percentile points — and at least one year classified as vegetation (top 40% of greenness) — are flagged as loss or gain. This approach is insensitive to absolute radiometric calibration between vintages.

All processing was done in Rust using the gdal crate. Source code: tools/canopy-loss.
